

Exercise 3: Prompt Transformation with Patterns

Objective: Strengthen weak prompts using patterns.

Take a vague or under-specified prompt (e.g., "Write about climate change.").

What is Climate Change?

Climate change refers to long-term alterations in Earth’s climate, particularly increases in global temperatures, shifts in weather patterns, and rising sea levels. While climate naturally fluctuates over centuries, human activities—especially the burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial emissions—have accelerated the process significantly.

Causes

Greenhouse Gas Emissions: Carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide trap heat in the atmosphere.

Deforestation: Reduces the Earth’s capacity to absorb CO₂, worsening the greenhouse effect.

Industrial and Agricultural Practices: Factories, power plants, and livestock contribute to emissions and environmental degradation.

Impacts

Rising temperatures leading to more heatwaves and wildfires.

Melting ice caps and glaciers, causing sea-level rise.

Extreme weather events like hurricanes, floods, and droughts.

Ecosystem disruption, threatening plant, animal, and human life.

Solutions

Shift to renewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro).

Reduce emissions through cleaner transportation, energy efficiency, and sustainable agriculture.

Reforestation and conservation to absorb carbon.

Promote global cooperation to meet climate targets and limit warming.

🌍 In short, climate change is one of the most urgent global challenges. While its effects are already visible, proactive efforts by individuals, governments, and organizations can mitigate damage and build a sustainable future.

Transform it using instructional or comparative patterns:

    Instructional: "Write a 200-word explanation of climate change for high school learners."

    Climate change is the long-term change in Earth’s weather patterns and temperatures. While the climate naturally changes over time, human activities have caused a faster and more dangerous type of climate change. The main cause is the increase of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, such as carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels like coal, oil, and gas. These gases trap heat, making the Earth warmer—a phenomenon called the greenhouse effect.

    Climate change leads to serious problems around the world. Temperatures are rising, causing more heatwaves and wildfires. Ice caps and glaciers are melting, which raises sea levels and threatens coastal cities. Extreme weather events, like hurricanes, floods, and droughts, are becoming more frequent and intense. Animals and plants are affected too, as their habitats change or disappear.

    Everyone can help reduce climate change. Switching to renewable energy like solar or wind, planting trees, and reducing waste are important steps. Governments and companies must also act to cut emissions and create sustainable solutions. Understanding climate change is crucial because it affects everyone’s future. By learning about it and taking action, high school students can become part of the global effort to protect the planet.

    Comparative: "Compare the effects of climate change on coastal vs inland regions in 5 bullet points."

    ere’s a clear comparison of climate change effects on coastal vs inland regions in 5 bullet points:

    Sea-Level Rise:

    Coastal: Rising seas cause flooding, erosion, and loss of land.

    Inland: Rarely affected directly, except for river flooding or drainage issues.

    Storms and Extreme Weather:

    Coastal: More frequent and stronger hurricanes, typhoons, and storm surges.

    Inland: Increased intensity of thunderstorms, heatwaves, and tornadoes.

    Temperature Changes:

    Coastal: Oceans moderate temperatures, so warming may be slower but increases humidity and heat index.

    Inland: Temperature extremes are more pronounced, with hotter summers and colder winters.

    Ecosystem Impact:

    Coastal: Saltwater intrusion threatens freshwater ecosystems, mangroves, and fisheries.

    Inland: Changes in rainfall affect agriculture, forests, and freshwater availability.

    Economic and Social Effects:

    Coastal: Damage to ports, tourism, and housing; increased displacement risk.

    Inland: Crop failures, water shortages, and heat-related health risks.
